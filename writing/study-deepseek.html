Study 2 · MoE + Latent Attention

DeepSeek-V2-Lite
on MI300X

Same MI300X, same methodology, a different architecture: MoE routing + Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA), 15.7B total / 2.4B active parameters. The question this study answers: does the dense-model playbook hold when the model itself is architecturally different?

Model: deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V2-Lite (BF16 / FP8)  ·  ShareGPT traces  ·  5 experiments
Experiment 01

Baseline & concurrency sweep

The same benchmark harness and metrics (TTFT / ITL / throughput) were rerun on DeepSeek-V2-Lite over ShareGPT conversational traces, then swept across concurrency to find the batching knee.

Baseline (batch=1): 182.0 tok/s, mean ITL 5.2 ms — the same HBM-bound signature as the dense model. Concurrency sweep scaled this to 6,990.5 tok/s at the knee (c ≈ 64), where higher concurrency also activates more distinct experts — coverage saturates alongside bandwidth amortization, unlike the dense model.

Experiment 02

KV-cache exhaustion

The same serving-boundary check was repeated: as concurrency rises, KV-cache pages compete with resident weights and activations for VRAM until vLLM begins preempting sequences.

At ISL=2048, the p99 TTFT cliff again lands at concurrency 256 — this boundary is a function of --gpu-memory-utilization and KV-page capacity, not the model's attention mechanism, so it reproduces identically across architectures.

Experiment 03

FP8 quantization

Only ~2.4B of the 15.7B total parameters are active per step (top-6/64 expert routing), so FP8's effect on active-byte traffic is proportionally smaller than on a dense model where all 8B params stream every step.

Peak throughput: 7,230.2 tok/s (+3.4%) vs. BF16 knee — far below the dense model's +55.0% — for +1.08% perplexity. Confirms FP8's leverage scales with how much weight traffic is actually streamed per step, not total parameter count.

Experiment 04

AITER decomposition — MoE vs. MLA

Enabling AITER flips MoE-grouped-GEMM, MLA-attention, MHA, LINEAR, and RMSNorm kernels all at once. A 4-arm leave-one-out ablation (full / no-MoE / no-MLA / off) isolated each kernel's contribution, cross-checked against rocprofv3 kernel-swap traces.

Whole-library AITER: 9,697.4 tok/s (+38.7%) vs. BF16 — the largest lever in either study — decomposed into +31.6% from MLA-attention kernels and +16.6% from MoE routing. The inverse of the dense study, where AITER was neutral: its surface area is architecture-dependent, concentrated in the grouped-MoE and latent-attention kernels this model actually has.

Experiment 05

Roofline interpretation

The same roofline framing applies: BF16 ridge ≈ 247 FLOP/byte, FP8 ridge ≈ 493 FLOP/byte. Decode sits deep below the ridge at low concurrency; AITER doesn't change arithmetic intensity — it raises the fraction of theoretical HBM bandwidth actually achieved.

Architecture changes where AITER's fused kernels have surface area (MoE + MLA here vs. none on dense Llama), but not the underlying roofline logic — decode remains HBM-bound, and every optimization here is ultimately fighting the same bandwidth ceiling from a different angle.
