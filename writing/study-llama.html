Study 1 · Dense Architecture

Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
on MI300X

A single AMD MI300X, vLLM 0.23.0, ROCm 7.2.3. Baseline → concurrency → KV-cache exhaustion → FP8 → AITER → composition → roofline — each step measured, attributed to a hardware resource, and proven with rocprofv3 / Omniperf evidence.

Model: NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (BF16 / FP8)  ·  7 experiments
Experiment 01

Baseline harness

Built a benchmark harness with vLLM's benchmark_serving.py against Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct resident in BF16, and defined TTFT, ITL, and end-to-end throughput as the measurement foundation for every subsequent experiment.

Single-stream (batch=1) baseline: 192.6 tok/s, mean ITL 4.9 ms. Roofline placed this at arithmetic intensity ≈ 1 FLOP/byte — 247× below the MI300X BF16 ridge — meaning >99% of peak TFLOP/s sat idle waiting on HBM weight reads.
vLLM 0.23.0 benchmark_serving.py BF16 TTFT / ITL
Experiment 02

Concurrency sweep

Continuous batching amortizes each HBM weight read across every concurrent request. A concurrency sweep found the batching knee — the point where more requests stop raising throughput — confirmed against rocprofv3 CU-occupancy traces.

Throughput scaled 192.6 → 5,595.3 tok/s (29×) from concurrency alone, saturating near c ≈ 246 where arithmetic intensity approaches the 247 FLOP/byte ridge. Mean ITL at peak: 77.6 ms. Marginal gain past the knee fell below 5% — bandwidth ceiling confirmed.
rocprofv3 CU occupancy continuous batching
Experiment 03

KV-cache exhaustion

Past the throughput knee, the ceiling shifts to HBM capacity: KV-cache pages compete with weights and activations for VRAM. Once pages fill, vLLM preempts sequences and forces a full prefill recompute on readmission.

At ISL=2048, p99 TTFT cliffs — a >50% single-step jump — at concurrency 256, confirmed by 'preempting' events in the vLLM server log. Above the cliff, p99 TTFT grows unboundedly: a hard SLO boundary independent of remaining throughput headroom.
KV-cache p99 TTFT preemption
Experiment 04

FP8 quantization

Decode is memory-bandwidth bound: each step loads the full 16.1 GB (8.03B params × 2B) weight set from HBM. Quantizing to FP8 halves that traffic to 8.0 GB per step and raises the FP8 MFMA compute roof from 1,307 → 2,615 TFLOP/s. Accuracy was validated with perplexity against the BF16 baseline.

Peak throughput at the knee: 8,671.5 tok/s (+55.0%) vs. BF16, mean ITL 49.7 ms (‑35.9%), for a +0.34% perplexity cost — the single highest-leverage knob in this study.
FP8 quantization HBM bandwidth perplexity
Experiment 05

AITER backend

AMD's AITER fused-kernel backend was toggled on and off (VLLM_ROCM_USE_AITER=1) while capturing rocprofv3 kernel traces, and the roofline model was used to predict the effect before measuring it.

Roofline predicted ~0% benefit on this dense architecture — compute <1% utilized at low concurrency, and AITER's kernels target MoE/MLA models, not standard attention. Confirmed empirically: ‑0.1% throughput, +1.7% ITL — within measurement noise.
AITER rocprofv3 roofline prediction
Experiment 06

FP8 + AITER composition

FP8 and AITER both relieve HBM bandwidth pressure — FP8 by shrinking bytes-per-step, AITER by using those bytes more efficiently. Hypothesis: stacking them is sub-additive because they compete for the same headroom.

Combined: 7,415.1 tok/s (+32.5%) vs. BF16 — well below the naive additive sum of +54.8%, confirming sub-additive stacking on a shared bottleneck.
FP8 AITER composition test
Experiment 07

Roofline analysis — prefill vs. decode

Omniperf roofline plots distinguish memory-bound from compute-bound kernels by plotting arithmetic intensity against achieved TFLOP/s relative to the BF16/FP8 ridge.

A long-prompt capture (ISL=7,680) landed at arithmetic intensity >7,680 FLOP/byte — far above the 247 FLOP/byte ridge, confirming prefill is compute-bound, the architectural inverse of memory-bound decode — motivating disaggregated prefill/decode serving as the next architectural step.
